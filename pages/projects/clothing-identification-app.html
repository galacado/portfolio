BSc Coursework · Pervasive Computing

Clothing Identification App

Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam

Created a device that helps visually impaired people identify the clothing they wish to wear.

pervasive computing image processing neural networks accessibility

The main objective of pervasive computing is to embed computational capability into everyday objects to make them effectively perform useful tasks, and ultimately, improve users’ lives. Collaborating with fellow students, we conceptualised and developed a specialised device designed to assist visually impaired individuals in identifying the clothing they wish to wear.

The interaction is simple: the user positions a piece of clothing in front of a camera, captures an image of it, and receives audible feedback detailing the type of clothing they’re holding — for example “blue t-shirt,” “red skirt,” “brown jacket,” or “black pants.”

How it works

The device uses morphological techniques to refine captured images, aiding shape recognition through edge detection, and enables the device to discern item colours as well. We used neural networks and conducted training to improve the device’s recognition capabilities, ensuring a more accurate and reliable result.

Project scope

Our project report included a thorough risk analysis, a review of relevant literature, a concept of operations outlining the intended device functioning, detailed system requirements, and storyboards illustrating key use case scenarios — alongside explanations of the techniques used, mock-ups, a full code overview, testing procedures, and an evaluation.
